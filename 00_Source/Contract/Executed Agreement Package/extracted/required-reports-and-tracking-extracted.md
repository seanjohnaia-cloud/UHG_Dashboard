# Required Reports and Tracking — Extracted Text

> Access layer only; original DOCX remains source of record.

1. Required Reports and Tracking
2. The Architect shall be responsible for tracking and reporting progress on all projects from the time that a proposal request is received to the time that the project is delivered, all deficiency list items are resolved, and closeout completion has occurred.
3. General:
4. Provide Reporting & Program Oversight – Submit periodic reports detailing project efficiency, risk assessments, and execution trends.
5. Support the Design Experience team in providing recommendations to support the evolution of the UHG design guidelines and ensure scalability across the portfolio
6. Annually:
7. An annual report which shall include a description of the goals and plans for the coming year (and a review of the performance of such goals and plans for the previous year). A report on Owner satisfaction surveys, an executive summary of the reports and proposed operating budgets if applicable.
8. Owner’s project related capital forecasting and year-end work in progress accrual process according to Owner’s policies and procedures
9. Quarterly:
10. Business Reviews
11. A.      Quarterly Business Review Using a format supplied by the Owner, the Architect shall prepare a Quarterly Business Review addressing the performance and activity of the Architect, in such detail and with such back-up as Owner shall require for presentation to the Owner’s stakeholders.  The report shall include information such as:
12. Detailed report of the cost-saving initiatives implemented and the resulting savings during the previous quarter and look ahead to future quarter.
13. Quarterly Project summary including any project risks
14. Summary of sustainability initiatives implemented on projects.
15. Summary of pilot initiatives implemented on projects.
16. Summary of Lessons Learned discovered during last quarter
17. Project Tracking Reports
18. Service Level Agreements (“SLAs”) and performance measures related to the terms and conditions of the Agreement
19. PROJECT TRACKING REPORTING: Architect shall provide Project tracking reporting, which shall include a summary of all projects by project phase. The report shall provide both a “dashboard” type summary supported by detailed project level information. The report shall include the following:
20. The total number of requests and projects handled. (Dashboard)
21. The percentage of requests that met the expected response times. (Dashboard)
22. Project name
23. Brief description
24. Percentage of Existing Conditions/ walls (etc.) reused per project (SD/DD Phase)
25. Scheduled Project phases
26. Project order and installation status (Current Project Status)
27. Quote requested, received dates and approval status
28. Potential project risk and proposed mitigation strategies
29. Project team leader and contact point
30. Number of Staff and their percentage of time spent on Owner’s account
31. Deficiency report
32. Project was or was not delivered according to the agreed-upon schedule, and budget at the final funding milestone (DD/CD Phase)
33. Cost Savings and applied strategies for cost savings
34. Schedule reduction (savings)
35. Architectural and MEP coordination issues/ misses
36. Number of Change Orders per project - broken down by type (detailed Owner scope change, unforeseen conditions, Errors and Omissions)
37. Number of RFIs per project- broken down by type (detailed Owner scope change, unforeseen conditions, Errors and Omissions)
38. Quarterly Business Reviews (QBR):
39. Architect shall be responsible for meeting the Key Performance Indicators. The Architect shall present progress toward KPIs at each 6-month Quarterly Business Review (QBR), Specifically in Q2 and Q4.
40. KPI Category
41. Goal
42. Project Delivery Timeliness
43. Deliver projects on or ahead of project schedule with % of projects delivered on time or early based on UHG MBO (Mgmt. by Objective).
44. Client Satisfaction
45. Achieve Net Promoter Score (NPS) satisfaction based on UHG MBO
46. Design Quality & Innovation
47. Maintain high design standards with limited RFIs or rework
48. Regulatory Compliance
49. Submit permits on time with minimal feedback (not more than one round of clarifications) on 98% or more of assigned projects.
50. Cost Alignment
51. Stay within approved construction budgets, with less than 3% variance between DD and CD milestones per project
52. Change Order Frequency
53. Minimize frequency of post-approval change orders <2% per project.
54. In addition to performance tracking, the Architect shall support the Owner’s strategic goals by delivering at least two (2) meaningful, supported by due diligence demonstrating viability, program recommendations in the six months prior to each bi-annual QBR. These recommendations may include – but are not limited to – initiatives that:
55. ·       Yield meaningful cost savings
56. ·       Improve program and/process efficiency or transparency
57. ·       Simplify UHG’s Design Guidelines
58. ·       Enhance quality or experience (team member, patient, or workplace)
59. ·       Identify relevant learning units for service lines
60. ·       Support broader UHG business objectives
61. Design Fee Reporting: (Refer to Exhibit J Attached)
62. Architect shall provide electronic reporting on Design fees by project to include Architectural and all consultant’s fees, inclusive of reimbursable costs and any additional services, quarterly and upon request by Owner.
63. Fees should be compared to budgeted fees/ proposal so that proposed vs actual costs are available.
64. Reporting format should match (or exceed) level of detail in Exhibit J and include any requested changes by the Owner.
65. Monthly:
66. At least once per month during the Project’s design phase, and additionally as reasonably requested by the Owner, the Architect shall provide an update to the Design Schedule. The Architect shall periodically advise the Owner of the status of the Project in relation to the Project Schedule and promptly notify the Owner in writing, in the event that, to the best of the Architect’s knowledge, information, and belief, the work is not proceeding in accordance with the Project Schedule approved in writing by the Owner, including, without limitation, (i) the progress of the Work being behind schedule, (ii) any delay in the critical path of the construction of the Project and (iii) the Work being performed out of sequence with such schedule.
