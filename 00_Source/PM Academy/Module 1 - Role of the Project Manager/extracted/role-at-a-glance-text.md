# PM Academy Module 1 — The Role of the Project Manager — Role at a Glance

> Access layer only. The PDF in `original/` remains the preserved source of record.

## Frame

"Your job isn't a discipline — it's the gaps between them. You are the one person answerable for whether the whole project comes together."

## Who You Are

**The Accountable Owner** — the one person answerable for the outcome, not just the tasks.

Habits to grow past:
- **The Coordinator** — tracks the tasks
- **The Designer** — chases the drawing
- **The Pleaser** — says yes to everything

→ **The Accountable Owner** — grow past the habit, own the outcome.

## The Goal — Why the Role Exists

Every project exists to create value. The Project Manager owns the conditions for value delivery.

```text
VALUE = PERFORMANCE × EXPERIENCE
```

It multiplies — it doesn't add.

- **Performance** — did we do the work well? Technical quality, value for scope and fees, schedule adherence.
- **Experience** — what was it like to work with us? Responsiveness, proactiveness, clear/accurate communication.

"It begins with curiosity about what value means to THIS client — the first act of Establishing Clarity."

## Where You Sit — Across All Three Streams

Three-way overlap (Client Experience / Team Leadership / Business Management):

- **PA** — owns the answer inside a discipline. "Makes it great."
- **PIC** — owns the client relationship & the firm's risk.
- **YOU (PM)** — own whether it all comes together. "You make it real."

"You balance all three at once — nobody else does."

Overlap notes: clarity of expectations drives predictability (Business Management ∩ Client Experience); well-managed scope protects trust and margin (Business Management); clear expectations reduce friction (Team Leadership ∩ Client Experience).

## How You Work — Three Moves Drive Five Outcomes

"Your job is in the gaps. In a gap, three things go wrong — so a PM drives those three things. Those three moves produce the five outcomes of PM excellence."

| Trigger | Move | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| When people get out of sync | **Establish Clarity** | Define scope, fee, schedule & contract. Run the kickoff. Nothing advances on assumption. |
| When work races ahead of decisions | **Control Advancement** | Manage coordination, budget & schedule. Surface variances early — not at the deadline. |
| When important things get sacrificed under pressure | **Protect Integrity** | Hold QA gates, price every change, protect quality and the fee. |

### The Five Outcomes (outcomes compound across all three moves)

1. **Defined Scope & Aligned Commitments** — scope, fee, schedule & contract settled before the team starts.
2. **Controlled, Predictable Delivery** — budgets, schedules & assignments actively managed; variances surfaced early.
3. **Protected Design & Technical Integrity** — code, coordination, BIM, constructability & QA guarded so design intent survives.
4. **Sustained Financial Health** — utilization & efficient execution protected; the PM drives firm profitability.
5. **Developed Teams & Collaborative Leadership** — leads the workflow, leverages SMEs, delegates & grows others.

## When — The Same Three Moves, Every Phase

| Move | Concentration | Content across phases |
|---|---|---|
| Establish Clarity | Front of project | Scope · fee · contract · kickoff · Design priorities |
| Control Advancement | Through the middle | Design coordination · monthly/phase budgets · schedule maintenance · team and client clarity |
| Protect Integrity | Throughout | QA gates · price every change · protect the fee |

Phases (five, shared with the companion maps): Pursuit & Contracting → Initiation & Planning → Design & Coordination → Construction Admin → Closeout & Growth.
